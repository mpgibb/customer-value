# Customer retention and finite-horizon value protocol

Decision: allocate a retention intervention to at most 20% of a cohort. Predictive churn risk, untreated contribution and incremental intervention value are different quantities. Model contractual churn as an absorbing cancellation; do not infer noncontractual churn from inactivity. Report discounted net contribution over twelve months, not unlimited lifetime value.

Original synthetic cohorts contain tenure, engagement, service friction, account tier and baseline monthly margin observed at scoring. Independent randomized intervention assignment has probability 0.5. Monthly survival and margins are observed for twelve months; oracle potential expectations are stored separately for evaluation only. The intervention costs $35 once at scoring. Discount monthly at (1.10)^(1/12). No acquisition costs or changes in intervention intensity are included.

Temporal design: model-fitting cohorts 0–23; validation cohorts 24–29; final scoring cohorts 36–47. The validation window is a diagnostic, not a tuning exercise. At the start of cohort 36, all training and validation twelve-month outcomes have matured. Freeze every model before the test cohorts are scored, then wait for the last cohort's full outcome window. This makes calendar-time information availability explicit. No random row split or partially matured customer outcome is used as a completed target.

Prediction: logistic discrete-time cancellation hazard with tenure/engagement/friction/tier features, nonlinear basis and duration; discounted survival-weighted margin forecast. Compare segment means for six-month churn and twelve-month contribution. Fit untreated prediction on control customers only. Report Brier score, calibration bins, log loss and finite-value MAE/bias on final controls. Survival-based value assumes baseline margin stays fixed, deliberately tested against margin variability.

Policy: three-fold out-of-fold outcome regressions on training cohorts create augmented inverse-propensity weighted treatment-effect scores using known assignment 0.5. Regress scores on prespecified features; target the highest positive expected net effect, up to 20% per cohort. Comparison policies target no one, everyone, highest predicted churn, or highest predicted untreated value (last two share the 20% budget). Evaluation on fresh randomized final cohorts uses a frozen-model doubly robust incremental contribution score versus no action, minus known treatment cost. Report cohort-cluster t intervals with 11 degrees of freedom and an explicit twelve-cluster limitation. Report oracle expected net gain and regret using separately stored expectations; the oracle is an unattainable evaluator benchmark, never a model input.

Robustness: stable response, no treatment effect, and reversed engagement/response relationship only in final cohorts. No tuning from any final scenario. Show all policies and adverse outcomes. Sensitivity reports $15/$35/$55 accounting costs for fixed selected policies and the same intervention; it is not a dose-response analysis. Bootstrap/cluster uncertainty is statistical evidence within the simulation, not evidence of client benefit.

Correctness: geometric discounted value by hand, known AIPW expectation under randomized assignment, budget bound and positive-score rule, oracle-column mutation leaves decisions unchanged, treatment effects do not change control prediction features, matured cutoff boundaries, fixed-seed reproducibility. Independent technical review remains pending.

References: Dudík, Langford & Li (2011), Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601 . Athey & Wager (2021), Policy Learning with Observational Data. https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02896 . This study uses a randomized design and a prespecified linear policy score, not the authors' full optimization algorithm or a claim to their theoretical guarantees.
